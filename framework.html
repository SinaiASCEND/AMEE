The core examination

24 maneuvers in 7 sections, performed for every patient whatever the complaint

Criteria describe observable behaviour, so different examiners reach the same judgment. All components present is full credit; any one missing is partial.

General Appearance And Vital Signs 5 maneuvers

1Observes general appearanceFullStudent observes the patient’s overall appearance including distress, posture, mental status, color, and general demeanor.PartialBrief glance OR insufficient assessment OR omission of key features.
2Assesses orientation (person, place, time)FullStudent should ask at least where the SP is (place) AND what time of day or date it is (time) AND ask the SP to state their own name (person). ALL ITEMS needed for full credit. Asking SP for name at top of encounter also counts.PartialAsked only one or two of the three orientation domains OR used closed-ended or leading questions.
3Measures blood pressureFullStudent placed the BP cuff on the right OR left arm AND the strands of cuff were positioned in the direction of the hands; no part of the gown was caught under cuff; must be correct on first try, without causing undue pain.PartialMinor technique issues OR had to repeat due to avoidable error OR caused minor discomfort.
4Measures heart rate (pulse)FullStudent used fingers and NOT thumb AND placed fingers on radial side of wrist; MUST count ≥15 seconds.PartialCounted <15 seconds OR used thumb OR improper location.
5Measures respiratory rateFullStudent quietly observes the patient’s breathing for at least 15 seconds and counts respirations.PartialObserved <15 seconds OR estimated without counting OR did not clearly observe movement.

Heent + Cranial Nerve Screen 4 maneuvers

6Inspects external eye and lidFullStudent inspected BOTH eyes AND used a light source to examine the external eye and eyelid for abnormalities.PartialStudent inspected only one eye OR did not use a light source OR examined the area too quickly to assess for abnormalities.
7Assesses pupillary light reflex (CN II/III)FullUsing a light source, student shines the light into the SP’s eye, one eye at a time, observing for pupillary constriction.PartialShined light into only one eye OR did not check consensual response OR used inadequate light/technique.
8Inspects oropharynx and dentitionFullStudent asked SP to open mouth, stick out tongue AND used a light source and tongue depressor (without causing gag).PartialStudent asked SP to open mouth but omitted tongue depressor OR failed to use a light source OR did not ask SP to stick out tongue.
9Palpates cervical lymph nodesFullStudent palpated at minimum in THREE of these areas: submental, submandibular, parotid, postauricular, occipital, anterior cervical, posterior cervical, deep cervical, and/or supraclavicular.PartialOnly 1–2 areas OR incorrect/superficial palpation.

Cardiac 2 maneuvers

10Palpates chest for cardiac point of maximal impulse (PMI)FullStudent palpated using fingers or palm along the left nipple area with the SP either lying down or sitting (if relevant, student examined under bra or asked permission to reposition left breast to accurately place fingers).PartialStudent palpated the chest but used an incorrect location (e.g., too medial or too high), did not adjust for breast tissue when needed, or used technique (e.g., tapping or pressing too lightly) that would not reliably detect the PMI.
11Auscultates heart in 4 areasFullStudent listened in the left AND right upper breastbone borders AND left lower breastbone border AND along nipple area (if relevant, under bra or SP repositions).PartialOnly 2–3 locations OR auscultated over clothing OR incorrect apex placement.

Pulmonary 3 maneuvers

12Percusses posterior chest (3 levels)FullStudent needs to percuss and COMPARE BOTH SIDES (moving from side to side) AND check in at least 3 levels.PartialStudent percussed fewer than 3 levels OR percussed only one side OR failed to alternate sides for comparison OR used incorrect technique (e.g., not striking finger properly).
13Auscultates posterior chest (3 levels)FullStudent listens moving side-to-side on BOTH sides AND checks at least 3 levels; MUST instruct deep breathing; MUST wait for exhale before moving.PartialFewer than 3 levels OR only one side OR unclear breath instruction OR moves without waiting for exhale.
14Auscultates anterior chest (2 levels)FullStudent listens moving side to side on BOTH sides AND checks at least 2 levels; MUST instruct deep breaths.PartialOnly one side OR fewer than 2 levels OR no breath instruction.

Abdomen 4 maneuvers

15Inspects abdomenFullStudent visually inspects the abdomen for contour, symmetry, skin changes, visible pulsations, or scars BEFORE performing any other abdominal exam maneuvers.PartialInspects AFTER other maneuvers OR brief/incomplete inspection.
16Auscultates abdomen before palpationFullStudent listened at any quadrant prior to palpating or percussing. MUST be done prior to palpating or percussing.PartialStudent auscultated the abdomen but did so after beginning palpation or percussion, OR auscultated too briefly or in an unclear location such that bowel sounds could not be reliably assessed.
17Performs light abdominal palpation (all 4 quadrants)FullStudent used light force with one or two hands AND MUST have palpated all 4 quadrants.PartialPalpated with moderate/deep pressure OR 1–3 quadrants only.
18Performs deep abdominal palpation (all 4 quadrants)FullStudent used deep downward force with one or two hands AND must have palpated all 4 quadrants.PartialOnly light pressure OR wrong technique OR 1–3 quadrants only.

Vascular 3 maneuvers

19Palpates posterior tibial pulses (bilateral)FullStudent MUST examine BOTH sides and feel for pulse along medial aspect of ankles.PartialOnly one side OR incorrect location OR improper pressure.
20Palpates dorsalis pedis pulses (bilateral)FullStudent MUST examine BOTH sides and feel for pulse along top of feet.PartialOnly one side OR incorrect location OR improper pressure.
21Palpates lower extremity for edemaFullStudent MUST palpate BOTH lower anterior legs directly on shin area over bone.PartialOne leg only OR palpates non-bone areas OR superficial pressure.

Neurologic 3 maneuvers

22Assesses gross UE motor strength (≥2 groups each arm)FullStudent assessed AT LEAST TWO muscle groups in BOTH upper extremities AGAINST RESISTANCE.PartialFewer than two muscle groups OR passive movement OR weak resistance.
23Assesses gross LE motor strength (≥2 groups each leg)FullStudent assessed AT LEAST TWO muscle groups in BOTH lower extremities AGAINST RESISTANCE.PartialFewer than two groups OR passive movement OR weak resistance.
24Assesses gaitFullStudent asks patient to walk and assesses natural gait.PartialAsked to walk but does not adequately observe gait.

Universal items counted separately from the 24

1Washes hands BEFORE examFullStudent washed hands AT ANY TIME BEFORE the physical with your full knowledge; using an instant hand sanitizer (e.g., Purell) IS also acceptable.PartialStudent did not sanitize hands before the physical exam but did so after entering the room and before leaving, or student made vague reference to prior handwashing without visible evidence.
2Observes proper drapingFullStudent used the drape effectively, exposing only one body part at a time. One prompt allowed.PartialMore than one prompt OR SP must adjust own drape → lower score.
3Washes hands AFTER examFullStudent washed hands AFTER the physical with your full knowledge; instant sanitizer acceptable.PartialNot performed.

The eight clusters

One cluster per encounter, selected by the presenting problem. Click to open.

Each cluster also restates the core maneuvers most relevant to that presentation; those are not repeated here. Musculoskeletal is organised by region — a patient draws the group for the joint involved, not all four. Optional add-ons need equipment, a chaperone, or a wider scope of practice.
Cardiac4 clusterchest pain · palpitations · syncope · oedema

Cluster maneuvers

1Auscultates THEN palpates carotids one side at a timeFullStudent did BOTH auscultation and palpation of the carotids one at a time AND used only the fingers (NOT the thumb). Student MUST auscultate before palpation.PartialStudent auscultated or palpated only one carotid artery OR used thumbs instead of fingers OR failed to perform auscultation before palpation OR performed on both sides simultaneously (unsafe).
2Estimates jugular venous pressure (JVP)FullStudent positions the patient at a 30–45° angle, uses tangential lighting, inspects the right side of the neck, identifies the highest point of jugular pulsation, and estimates the vertical distance from the sternal angle in centimeters.PartialStudent positions the patient at an incorrect angle, uses overhead or no lighting, inspects the wrong area, or estimates the vertical height inaccurately (e.g., from the clavicle or not perpendicular to the sternal angle).
3Auscultates heart sounds in left lateral decubitus (low-pitched mitral stenosis)FullStudent asks the patient to roll onto their left side, uses the bell of the stethoscope at the apex, and listens silently for 5 seconds.PartialStudent skips the position, uses the wrong tool or location, or does not listen silently for 5 seconds.
4Auscultates heart sounds with patient leaning forward (aortic regurgitation)FullStudent asks the patient to sit up, exhale, and lean forward, uses the diaphragm of the stethoscope at the left sternal border or base, and listens silently for 5 seconds.PartialStudent misses a step (no forward lean or exhale), uses the wrong tool or location, or doesn’t listen silently for 5 seconds.
Pulmonary6 cluster+4 optionaldyspnoea · cough · wheeze · chest tightness

Cluster maneuvers

1Assesses posterior chest expansionFullStudent needs to palpate BOTH SIDES using the sides of the hands or palms AND check in at least ONE level overlying the posterior lungs to get the point. Students MUST test both sides simultaneously. MUST ask patient to take a deep breath.PartialStudent palpated only one side OR failed to compare sides simultaneously OR omitted breath instruction OR did not use appropriate hand positioning.
2Performs posterior chest for tactile fremitusFullStudent needs to palpate and COMPARE BOTH SIDES using the sides of the hands or palms (WHILE the SP is asked to repeat a word or phrase such as ‘ninety-nine’ or ‘toy boat’) AND check in at least 3 levels in the back to get the point. Students may test both sides simultaneously.PartialStudent checked fewer than 3 levels OR palpated only one side OR omitted the spoken phrase OR used palms improperly without comparing sides.
3Percusses posterior chestFullStudent needs to percuss and COMPARE BOTH SIDES (moving from side to side) AND check in at least 3 levels.PartialStudent percussed fewer than 3 levels OR percussed only one side OR failed to alternate sides for comparison OR used incorrect technique (e.g., not striking finger properly).
4Assesses for egophonyFullStudent instructs the patient to say ‘ee’ while auscultating at least 3 posterior lung fields on each side using the diaphragm of the stethoscope. Student listens for any change in sound quality (e.g., ‘ee’ to ‘ay’).PartialStudent tests fewer than 3 posterior fields per side, does not clearly instruct the patient to say ‘ee’, or omits use of the diaphragm of the stethoscope.
5Assesses for whispered pectoriloquyFullStudent instructs the patient to whisper ‘1-2-3’ or ‘toy boat’ while auscultating at least 3 posterior lung fields per side using the diaphragm of the stethoscope.PartialStudent tests fewer than 3 posterior fields per side, gives unclear or no instruction to whisper, or fails to use the diaphragm.
6Assesses for CVA tenderness (used when pulmonary vs renal pain overlap)FullStudent locates the costovertebral angle on both sides, places one hand flat over the angle, and uses a closed fist to percuss on the flat hand. Student asks the patient if it is painful after each side is tested.PartialStudent strikes the back without using a flat hand, presses or taps in the wrong location, tests only one side, or strikes too forcefully or too lightly.

Optional add-ons equipment, chaperone, or wider scope of practice

1Inspects for accessory muscle useFullStudent inspects the patient’s anterior and lateral chest for use of accessory muscles (sternocleidomastoid, scalene, intercostal retractions) while the patient breathes normally. Student identifies increased work of breathing if present.PartialStudent glances at the chest but does not clearly assess muscle use or inspects only briefly without observing several breaths. Does not comment on the presence or absence of increased work of breathing.
2Assesses for wheezing with forced expirationFullStudent instructs the patient to take a full breath in and exhale forcefully while the student auscultates the anterior and posterior lung fields using the diaphragm of the stethoscope, listening specifically for wheezing.PartialStudent does not instruct a forceful exhalation, auscultates only one area, uses the bell instead of the diaphragm, or listens too briefly to assess for wheezing.
3Measures oxygen saturationFullStudent applies the pulse oximeter correctly to a finger (or alternate site if needed), ensures proper sensor placement, waits for a stable reading, and states the oxygen saturation and pulse displayed.PartialStudent places the sensor incorrectly, fails to wait for a stable reading, does not confirm the displayed value, or misinterprets the number shown.
4Measures peak flow (asthma)FullStudent sets the peak flow meter to zero, instructs the patient to take a maximal inhalation, seal lips tightly around the mouthpiece, and blow out as hard and fast as possible. Student repeats the maneuver three times and records the highest value.PartialStudent does not reset the meter, provides unclear instructions, allows air leaks around the mouthpiece, performs fewer than three attempts, or fails to record the highest value.
Gastrointestinal9 cluster+5 optionalabdominal pain · nausea · altered bowel habit

Cluster maneuvers

1Palpates liver edgeFullStudent palpates along the area just below the right rib cage.PartialStudent palpates too high or too lateral, used brief or shallow pressure, or did not coordinate palpation with the patient’s breath in (e.g., palpated before or after inspiration).
2Palpates spleenFullThree possible ways to get full credit: (1) With the SP lying flat, used bimanual exam with one hand posteriorly along the left lower rib cage and the other hand along the left lower rib cage anteriorly, OR (2) With the SP lying flat, used the hooking technique with fingers of both hands hooking under the left lower rib cage… OR (3) Palpated just under the left lower rib cage with the SP on the right lateral position.PartialStudent palpates near the left costal margin only without using a validated approach, omits deep inspiration, uses one hand instead of bimanual technique, or performs a maneuver that would not effectively assess spleen enlargement.
3Performs Murphy’s signFullStudent palpates just below the right costal margin at the mid-clavicular line, instructs the patient to take a deep breath, and observes for discomfort or inspiratory pause.PartialStudent palpates in the wrong location, applies pressure without coordinating with inspiration, or gives unclear instructions for breathing.
4Assesses for McBurney’s point tenderness and reboundFullStudent locates McBurney’s point (1/3 of the distance from the anterior superior iliac spine to the umbilicus), applies firm pressure, then releases abruptly and asks the patient if pain is present.PartialStudent palpates in an incorrect location, performs slow or incomplete release, or gives unclear or no instructions when asking about pain.
5Assesses for rebound tendernessFullStudent presses gently but firmly into the lower abdomen, then quickly releases, and asks the patient if pain worsens when letting go.PartialStudent palpates in an incorrect location, performs slow or incomplete release, or gives unclear or no instructions when asking about pain.
6Performs Rovsing’s signFullStudent presses into the left lower quadrant and asks the patient if they feel pain in the right lower quadrant.PartialStudent presses into the left lower quadrant but asks about local pain only, or presses into the wrong quadrant, or presses too briefly or gently.
7Performs psoas signFullStudent either: (1) Instructs the patient to raise the right leg against resistance, OR (2) Passively extends the right leg with the patient lying on their left side and asks if it causes right lower quadrant pain.PartialStudent performs the maneuver with poor technique (e.g., applies resistance to the wrong leg, extends the wrong leg, or positions patient incorrectly), or asks about general discomfort rather than specifying right lower quadrant pain.
8Performs obturator signFullWith the patient lying supine, student flexes the patient’s right hip and knee to 90°, then gently rotates the leg internally and externally at the hip while asking whether this maneuver causes right lower quadrant pain.PartialStudent performs the maneuver incorrectly (e.g., does not flex the hip/knee properly, rotates the leg minimally or too quickly, or rotates at the knee instead of the hip), tests only one movement, or fails to ask specifically about right lower quadrant pain.
9Assesses for CVA tendernessFullStudent locates the costovertebral angle (CVA) on both sides, places one hand flat over the angle, and uses a closed fist to percuss on the flat hand. Student asks the patient if it is painful after each side is tested.PartialStudent strikes the back without using a flat hand, presses or taps in the wrong location, tests only one side, or strikes too forcefully or too lightly.

Optional add-ons equipment, chaperone, or wider scope of practice

1Performs rectal examFullStudent explains the procedure clearly, ensures appropriate draping, positions the patient correctly (left lateral decubitus or standing leaning forward), applies lubricant, and gently inserts the finger into the rectum. Student palpates circumferentially for masses, tenderness, sphincter tone, and notes presence of stool or blood. Student removes the finger gently and inspects the glove.PartialStudent gives incomplete explanation, uses inadequate draping, positions the patient incorrectly, inserts a finger abruptly or without lubrication, assesses incompletely (e.g., no circumferential palpation), or fails to inspect the glove after withdrawal.
2Performs stool guaiac test (FOBT)FullStudent applies a small sample of stool to the guaiac card, adds developer solution, waits the appropriate time, and interprets the color change accurately as positive or negative. Student explains findings to the patient if needed.PartialStudent applies stool incorrectly (too much or too little), fails to use developer solution, does not wait the required time before reading the result, or misinterprets the color change.
3Performs hernia examFullStudent inspects the groin for asymmetry or bulging, asks the patient to stand, and palpates the inguinal canal (following the spermatic cord in males or along the inguinal ligament in females). Student asks the patient to cough or bear down while palpating for an impulse indicating a hernia.PartialStudent inspects but does not palpate, palpates incorrectly (e.g., wrong location), omits the cough/strain maneuver, or performs the exam with poor draping or unclear explanation.
4Performs Carnett’s signFullStudent identifies a point of maximal abdominal tenderness, maintains gentle pressure on the area, and asks the patient to lift their head and shoulders off the table (or raise straight legs). Student notes whether tenderness increases (suggesting abdominal wall pain) or decreases (intra-abdominal source).PartialStudent does not maintain pressure on the tender point during the maneuver, gives unclear instructions, fails to observe the patient’s response, or misinterprets increased/decreased tenderness.
5Performs pelvic exam (if indicated)FullStudent explains the procedure clearly, ensures adequate draping, uses appropriate positioning (lithotomy), inspects external genitalia, performs speculum exam with proper technique, and performs bimanual exam assessing uterus, adnexa, and cervical motion tenderness. Student maintains patient comfort, uses lubricant appropriately, and communicates throughout.PartialStudent provides inadequate explanation, uses poor draping, inserts the speculum incorrectly or too forcefully, does not visualize the cervix, performs incomplete bimanual exam (e.g., does not assess adnexa), or fails to monitor patient comfort.
Neuro-vestibular20 clusterdizziness · vertigo · headache · weakness

Cluster maneuvers

1Examines UE reflexes: bicepsFullStudent MUST examine BOTH biceps reflexes with the patient’s arm supported either on the student’s forearm or on the patient’s own thigh.PartialStudent examines only one side, supports the arm incorrectly, or strikes in an incorrect location.
2Examines UE reflexes: tricepsFullStudent MUST examine BOTH triceps reflexes with the arm supported by the student’s hand or on the patient’s thigh, striking the correct tendon.PartialStudent examines only one side, provides inadequate support, or strikes above or below the correct tendon location.
3Examines knee reflexes (patellar)FullStudent MUST examine BOTH patellar reflexes with the legs freely dangling and strikes the patellar tendon correctly.PartialStudent examines only one side, does not allow the legs to dangle, or misses the tendon.
4Examines ankle reflexes (Achilles)FullStudent MUST examine BOTH sides AND needs to lift/support the foot before hitting the Achilles tendon.PartialStudent examines only one side, fails to support the foot, or strikes outside the tendon.
5Examines plantar reflex (Babinski)FullStudent MUST examine BOTH sides, tracing a firm stroke in a “C” or “L” pattern along the lateral foot and across the great toe.PartialStudent tests only one foot, uses an incorrect stroke pattern, leaves socks on, or applies pressure that is too light or too forceful.
6Tests for station (Romberg)FullStudent asks the patient to stand with feet together and eyes closed, observing for sway or loss of balance.PartialStudent has the patient stand with feet apart, fails to request eye closure, or provides unclear instructions.
7Assesses cerebellar function (rapid alternating movements, finger-to-nose test, heel-to-shin test)FullStudent assesses cerebellar function using rapid alternating movements, finger-to-nose, or heel-to-shin, and performs the maneuver on both sides.PartialStudent performs testing on only one side, provides unclear instructions, or performs too few repetitions for assessment.
8Assesses vibration sense (UE + LE)FullStudent uses a vibrating tuning fork on a bony prominence of one finger and one toe, asking when vibration starts and stops.PartialStudent tests only one limb, places the fork on soft tissue, or does not verify start/stop sensation.
9Assesses proprioception sense (UE + LE)FullStudent holds the sides of a finger and a toe, moves each up or down with the patient’s eyes closed, and asks the patient to identify the direction.PartialStudent tests only one limb, holds the digit incorrectly (top/bottom), or provides unclear movement or questioning.
10Assesses toe walk, heel walk, and tandem gaitFullStudent asks the patient to 1) walk on toes, 2) walk on heels, and 3) perform tandem gait (heel-to-toe walking in a straight line).PartialStudent performs only one or two components, gives unclear instructions, or does not observe adequately.
11Assesses for nuchal rigidity (only when indicated by headache, fever, meningismus)FullStudent gently lifts the patient’s head, attempting to bring the chin toward the chest, while observing for stiffness or resistance.PartialStudent performs the maneuver without supporting the head properly, lifts too abruptly, or does not bring the chin toward the chest fully.
12Performs Kernig test (only when indicated)FullStudent flexes the patient’s hip and knee to 90°, then attempts to straighten the leg while observing for pain or resistance.PartialStudent performs the maneuver on only one side, fails to maintain hip at 90°, or extends the leg too quickly or incompletely.
13Performs Brudzinski test (only when indicated)FullStudent lifts the patient’s head toward the chest and observes for involuntary flexion of the hips and knees.PartialStudent lifts the head too quickly, fails to observe the legs, or misses the hip/knee response.
14Performs Dix–Hallpike maneuver (Gold standard for BPPV)FullStudent turns the patient’s head 45° to one side, supports the head and shoulders, and brings the patient rapidly from sitting to supine with the head extended below the table edge. Student observes the eyes for nystagmus and asks about vertigo. Maneuver is repeated on the opposite side.PartialStudent positions the head incorrectly, lowers the patient too slowly or without head extension, fails to observe the eyes for nystagmus, does not ask about vertigo, or performs the maneuver on only one side.
15Performs HINTS Exam (Head-Impulse, Nystagmus, Test of Skew — for acute vestibular syndrome)FullStudent performs (1) Head-Impulse Test by asking the patient to fix gaze while the head is turned rapidly and notes corrective saccades; (2) observes nystagmus in primary, left, and right gaze; and (3) performs alternating cover-uncover test to assess for skew deviation. Student interprets central vs peripheral pattern.PartialStudent performs fewer than all three components, performs them incorrectly (e.g., slow head thrust), does not instruct the patient properly, fails to observe corrective saccades, or provides no interpretation.
16Assesses nystagmus (Spontaneous or gaze-evoked)FullStudent asks the patient to look straight ahead and then in left, right, upward, and downward gaze while the student observes for spontaneous, gaze-evoked, or positional nystagmus and notes direction, fatigability, and symmetry.PartialStudent views only primary gaze, does not assess gaze in multiple directions, observes briefly without identifying type or direction, or gives no comment on findings.
17Performs orthostatic vital signs (For syncope, dizziness, presyncope)FullStudent measures blood pressure and pulse after the patient has been supine for at least 3 minutes, then again after standing for 1 minute and 3 minutes, assessing for orthostatic drop in BP or compensatory rise in pulse.PartialStudent measures vitals only once, measures immediately after standing without waiting, does not record pulse, or inconsistently times the measurements.
18Assesses pronator drift (Upper motor neuron sign)FullStudent asks the patient to hold both arms forward with palms up and eyes closed for 10–20 seconds while observing for pronation, downward drift, or asymmetry.PartialStudent positions arms incorrectly, does not ask the patient to close eyes, observes for too short a period, or fails to identify subtle drift.
19Performs light-touch sensory examFullStudent lightly touches symmetric areas on the upper and lower extremities, comparing right and left sides and asking the patient whether sensation feels the same.PartialStudent tests only one limb, touches too firmly or inconsistently, or does not compare both sides or ask the patient about differences.
20Performs pinprick sensory exam (If equipment allowed)FullStudent uses a disposable pin or neurotip to test sharp sensation on symmetric locations of upper and lower extremities, comparing right and left and asking the patient whether the sensation is sharp and symmetric.PartialStudent tests only one location or limb, uses dull pressure inconsistently, fails to compare sides, or does not ask about differences.

Shoulder (IPROMS) 9 maneuvers

1Inspects shoulder jointFullStudent visually inspects both shoulders, comparing both sides, with the patient sitting or standing and both shoulders exposed.PartialStudent inspects only one shoulder, performs the inspection through clothing, or gives a cursory glance without clearly observing structure or symmetry.
2Palpates shoulder jointFullStudent palpates the clavicle, acromioclavicular (AC) joint, bicipital groove, and scapular spine on both sides, asking about tenderness.PartialStudent palpates fewer than two landmarks, palpates only one shoulder, or does so with poor technique.
3Assesses shoulder ROM — flexion/extensionFullStudent asks the patient to raise both arms forward and overhead (flexion) and then extend them behind the body.PartialStudent performs only one movement or tests only one arm.
4Assesses shoulder ROM — abduction/adductionFullStudent asks the patient to lift both arms out to the side and overhead (abduction) and then bring them across the body (adduction).PartialPerforms only one direction, tests only one arm, or allows compensation.
5Assesses shoulder ROM — internal/external rotationFullStudent asks the patient to perform internal rotation (hand behind back) and external rotation (arms abducted to 90°, elbows bent, rotating forearms backward).PartialTests only one direction or one arm, gives unclear instructions, or positions improperly.
6Special Maneuver - Performs drop arm testFullStudent lifts the patient’s arm to shoulder level and asks the patient to slowly lower it while observing for control.PartialTests only one side, gives unclear instructions, or does not watch descent carefully.
7Special Maneuver - Performs Neer testFullStudent gently lifts the patient’s straight arm forward and upward with the palm facing down while stabilizing the shoulder.PartialTests one arm, bends elbow, or fails to stabilize the shoulder.
8Special Maneuver - Performs modified Hawkins testFullStudent lifts the patient’s arm in front to shoulder level, bends the elbow, and rotates the forearm downward while stabilizing the arm.PartialWrong position, tests one side only, or moves too quickly.
9Special Maneuver - Performs empty can testFullStudent asks the patient to lift arms to shoulder height angled forward, thumbs down, and resists downward pressure.PartialImproper hand position, tests one side, or inadequate resistance.

2Palpates knee jointFullStudent palpates both knees, feeling around patella, joint lines, and soft tissue.PartialPalpates one knee only or misses key areas.
3Assesses knee ROM — flexion/extensionFullStudent asks patient to bend (flex) and straighten (extend) the knee fully on both sides.PartialTests only one movement or one leg.

7Special Maneuver - Performs valgus (MCL) stress testFullStudent applies outward pressure on lower leg while stabilizing thigh, testing medial ligament.PartialTests one side or misdirects pressure.

2Palpates hip jointFullStudent palpates anterior hip joint region and greater trochanter bilaterally.PartialPalpates only one side or incorrect structures.
3Assesses hip ROM — flexion/extensionFullPatient bends forward to touch toes (flexion) and leans backward (extension) with support.PartialPerforms only one direction or poor instruction.

HEENT7 cluster+10 optionalsore throat · ear pain · visual change · sinus pain

Cluster maneuvers

1Assesses visual acuity using Snellen cards or wall chartFullStudent assessed both eyes (one at a time) AND obliterated vision on the eye not being tested. If SP is wearing glasses, they should be instructed to keep glasses on. Should be held at 14 inches, unless otherwise noted on the card. Student can use the wall chart instead and should place the patient at the appropriate distance as listed on the chart.PartialStudent assessed only one eye OR did not occlude the opposite eye OR used incorrect distance (too close or too far) OR forgot to instruct SP to wear glasses when applicable.
2Assesses CN II (visual fields)FullPatient SHOULD BE at eye level. Instructs patient to stare at a fixed point (e.g., student’s nose). With student’s fingers in the periphery of the patient’s vision and halfway between him/her and the patient, the student should: 1) gradually move the finger towards the center of vision, until it is perceived and the patient is asked to identify or point to the moving fingers OR 2) holds up a number of fingers and asks the patient to identify the total number of fingers held up. The visual fields of both eyes should be checked in all four quadrants.PartialStudent assessed fewer than four quadrants OR failed to instruct the patient to fix gaze OR performed the maneuver too quickly or too close to the face, limiting reliable field testing.
3Uses ophthalmoscope correctly (eyes)FullStudent assessed both eyes AND used his/her right eye on SP’s right eye and his/her left eye on SP’s left eye UNLESS they are using a panoptic (done in most respectful manner possible paying attention to the comfort of the patient). Light from the ophthalmoscope MUST be illuminating SP’s eye (as opposed to cheek, brow, etc.).PartialStudent assessed only one eye OR used incorrect eye-hand alignment OR did not visibly illuminate SP’s eye directly (e.g., light shone on face but not through pupil).
4Uses otoscope correctly (ears)FullStudent examined both ears AND pulled back the ear to straighten the ear canal AND braced the instrument against the head using the instrument hand, without causing physical pain.PartialStudent examined only one ear OR omitted pulling back the ear OR failed to brace the instrument OR did not position the light into the canal properly, making visualization unclear.
5Palpates the sinuses for tendernessFullStudent uses fingertips to gently palpate the frontal sinuses (above the eyebrows) and the maxillary sinuses (under the cheekbones) on both sides and asks the patient if either area is tender.PartialStudent palpates only one set of sinuses (frontal or maxillary), or does not palpate both sides, or forgets to ask the patient about tenderness, or fails to palpate using fingertips with appropriate pressure.
6Assesses CN IX & X (gag reflex/soft palate elevation)FullStudent asked SP to open mouth AND say “ah” AND used a tongue depressor and a light source.PartialMisses one step (e.g., no illumination or failed inspection).
7Palpates thyroid gland from EITHER front or sideFullStudent MUST palpate the area BELOW the larynx (Adam’s apple) with fingers AND needs to ask the patient to swallow during palpation (student does not cause discomfort or make the SP feel like he/she is choking).PartialWrong area palpated, didn’t ask to swallow, or excessive pressure.

Optional add-ons equipment, chaperone, or wider scope of practice

1Assesses nasal patency (one nostril at a time)FullStudent occludes one nostril at a time with a finger, asks the patient to sniff through the opposite nostril, and compares airflow bilaterally.PartialStudent tests only one nostril OR gives unclear instructions OR does not compare airflow between sides.
2Inspects nasal cavity with lightFullStudent uses a light source to inspect the anterior nasal cavity on both sides, visualizing the septum and mucosa clearly for color, swelling, discharge, or lesions.PartialStudent inspects only one side OR uses inadequate lighting OR does not visualize both the septum and mucosa.
3Assesses tonsils (erythema, exudate, enlargement)FullStudent uses a tongue depressor and light source, asks the patient to open their mouth widely, and inspects both tonsils for size, symmetry, erythema, or exudate.PartialStudent inspects briefly OR inspects only one side OR uses poor lighting OR fails to use a tongue depressor when needed.
4Performs Weber testFullStudent places a vibrating tuning fork at the midline of the forehead, asks whether the sound is heard equally or better in one ear, and confirms understanding of lateralization.PartialStudent places tuning fork incorrectly OR does not confirm lateralization OR fails to verify equal perception.
5Performs Rinne test (AC vs BC)FullStudent places a vibrating tuning fork on the mastoid process, asks patient to say when sound stops, then moves it in front of the ear canal to compare air vs bone conduction.PartialStudent performs only one portion of the test OR places tuning fork incorrectly OR gives unclear instructions OR does not compare bone and air conduction.
6Assesses trismus (jaw opening)FullStudent asks the patient to open their mouth fully and evaluates jaw opening for restriction or pain, observing symmetry and ease of movement.PartialStudent gives unclear instructions OR briefly inspects without assessing opening OR fails to note limitation or discomfort.
7Assesses cervical range of motionFullStudent asks the patient to flex, extend, rotate, and laterally bend the neck, observing for symmetry, limitation, or discomfort in each movement.PartialStudent assesses fewer than all planes of motion OR gives unclear instructions OR does not observe movement adequately.
8Inspects conjunctiva and scleraFullStudent gently retracts lower eyelids and uses a light source to inspect the conjunctiva and sclera bilaterally for color, injection, or pallor.PartialStudent inspects only one eye OR does not retract the eyelid adequately OR uses poor lighting.
9Assesses for periorbital edemaFullStudent inspects both eyelids for swelling, compares sides, and gently palpates the periorbital area for firmness or pitting.PartialStudent inspects only one side OR palpates incorrectly OR does not compare sides.
10Assesses for nystagmus (brief screen)FullStudent asks the patient to look straight ahead and in all cardinal directions while observing eye movements carefully for spontaneous or gaze-evoked nystagmus.PartialStudent inspects only primary gaze OR does not assess all directions OR observes too briefly to detect nystagmus.
Genitourinary6 cluster+8 optionaldysuria · pelvic pain · discharge · urinary change

Cluster maneuvers

1Assesses for CVA tendernessFullStudent locates the costovertebral angle (CVA) on both sides, places one hand flat over the angle, and uses a closed fist to percuss on the flat hand. Student asks the patient if it is painful after each side is tested.PartialStudent strikes the back without using a flat hand, presses or taps in the wrong location, tests only one side, or strikes too forcefully or too lightly.
2Palpates inguinal lymph nodesFullStudent palpates the inguinal crease bilaterally using firm but gentle pressure to assess lymph node size, tenderness, or enlargement.PartialPalpates only one side OR uses superficial pressure OR unclear technique.

5Assesses bladder distentionFullStudent palpates from the umbilicus downward toward the pelvis, using firm pressure to identify a distended bladder.PartialPalpates incorrectly OR briefly OR with insufficient pressure.
6Evaluates urethral meatusFullStudent inspects the urethral meatus directly using proper retraction of tissue to assess for erythema, discharge, or lesions.PartialFails to retract tissue adequately OR incomplete visualization OR inspects too briefly.
